Backlog Prioritisation Hub

One workspace for refinement, prioritisation, sprint planning and delivery oversight. It brings Jira demand from two product lines together, ranks it on evidence and records every decision.

In useEfficioBuilt soloSince June 2026
Problem
Over 200 tickets across two products, bugs mixed with features, no consistent ranking and no record of why.
Built
A Jira synced hub with separate paths for bugs and features, evidence based scoring, voting and a full audit trail.
Result
Shorter refinement, faster triage and less debate. Now the refinement group's primary tool.

What changed

  • Refinement sessions are shorter, with less time spent debating order.
  • Triage is faster, because every ticket arrives classified and scored.
  • The engagement team, who work directly with users, now has a voice in priorities.
  • Roadmap themes are backed by demand evidence I can take to senior leaders.

Leadership overview

Sprint health, scope changes, blockers, decisions needing attention and roadmap demand signals.

Backlog

A board per product line, moving tickets from first pass to review, estimate and proposed sprint, with a full ticket drawer.

Feature Insights

Ranked roadmap themes, ideas kept apart from validated demand, voting results and delivery history.

Delivery and Jira

Release briefs, sprint scope changes and churn, sync settings and data quality reviews.

  1. 1
    Sync

    Pulls tickets and sprint history from Jira, with a separate query for each product line so one failure never affects the other.

  2. 2
    Classify

    Separates bugs from features using the Jira issue type. Uncertain cases go to a human review queue.

  3. 3
    Score

    Bugs are ordered by Jira severity, and critical bugs skip the queue as interrupt work. Features are scored on evidence, then adjusted by effort and MoSCoW.

  4. 4
    Vote

    The engagement team votes anonymously, with an optional reason. Votes inform priorities and roadmap signals.

  5. 5
    Plan

    Proposes the next sprint while keeping capacity split across product areas, so no one area takes the whole sprint.

What a feature is scored on

  • User or client impact, and how many people it reaches.
  • Roadmap alignment, which only counts as high with a named source such as a roadmap theme or client commitment.
  • Client sensitivity, revenue, retention or adoption value.
  • Efficiency gains and risk reduction.
  • Then moderated by delivery effort and Jira priority, and adjusted by MoSCoW.

Trust over automation

  • No generative AI in the scoring. Fixed, explainable rules make every rank auditable.
  • Votes advise but never silently override the formal score or Jira priority.
  • Jira stays the source of truth for tickets and sprints. The hub owns refinement decisions.
  • Manual overrides survive every Jira refresh and are recorded in the audit history.

Guardrails

  • A feature cannot be ranked until it has an estimate and an explicit MoSCoW choice.
  • Ideas are kept separate from validated demand.
  • A failed Jira sync never deletes tickets, and archived tickets are kept for reporting.
  • Sprint changes use explicit actions, not free drag and drop, so scope changes are traceable.

Feature I added: anonymous voting

The engagement team works directly with users. Voting is optional: they can vote up or down on any active ticket and add an anonymous reason. Net support feeds the roadmap signals in Feature Insights, so what users ask for is visible when priorities are set. One vote per browser keeps it quick, with no login needed.
